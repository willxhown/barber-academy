Lesson 1: Barber Tools and Supplies

Lesson 1 of 9

Before cutting hair, you must know what each tool does. Below are the basic tools you will use every day in the shop.

1. Hair Clippers & Guards

Used to cut large amounts of hair fast and blend fade lines using guard numbers (#0.5 to #4).

2. Outlining Trimmer

Used to make sharp straight lines on the temples, sideburns, and back of the neck.

3. Foil Shaver

Cleans the neck down to smooth skin (0.0mm) without cutting or scratching the client.

4. Cutting Shears (Scissors)

Used to trim and cut hair on the top of the head and blend with the comb.

5. Straight Razor (Shavette)

Uses a clean, disposable razor blade to clean necklines and beard lines with shaving gel.

6. Barber Combs & Clips

Wide comb for detangling, taper comb for fading, and clips to hold top hair out of the way.

Lesson 2: Cleanliness, Hygiene & Safety

Lesson 2 of 9

Clean tools prevent scalp infections, dandruff, and pimples from spreading from one client to another.

3 Rules for Every Haircut:

  1. Always Use a Neck Strip: Never let the black vinyl cape touch the client's bare skin. Wrap a clean white paper neck strip around their neck first.
  2. Barbicide Soak (10 Minutes): Wash combs and scissors with soap and water to remove loose hair, then soak them in blue Barbicide liquid for 10 minutes between clients.
  3. New Blade for Every Person: Throw away the used razor blade into a plastic red biohazard box immediately after the haircut. Never reuse a blade on two different people.
Stop! Do Not Cut If You See:

If a client has open sores, bleeding pimples, or circular red itchy patches (ringworm), politely tell them they need to visit a doctor first before getting a haircut.

Lesson 3: The 4 Main Parts of the Head

Lesson 3 of 9

To talk with your trainer and follow haircut steps, you must know where these 4 main areas are on every client's head.

  • 1. Top (Crown & Fringe): The hair on top of the head that you cut with scissors and comb forward, up, or back.
  • 2. Parietal Ridge (The Curve): The widest curved bone along the sides of the head. This connects the top hair to your side fade.
  • 3. Occipital Bone (The Bump): The round bump you feel at the back of the skull. Low fades stay under this bump; mid fades go right across it.
  • 4. Nape: The bottom edge of the hairline right above the neck.

Lesson 4: How to Shampoo and Blow-Dry

Lesson 4 of 9

Step-by-Step Hair Wash:

  1. Check Water Heat: Always test the warm water on your own wrist first so you do not burn the client's scalp.
  2. Use Finger Pads: Rub shampoo using the soft pads of your fingers in circles. Never scratch with sharp fingernails.
  3. Rinse Clean: Cup your hand over their forehead so warm water does not drip into the client's eyes.

3 Rules for Blow-Drying:

  1. Blow Downward: Always point the blower tip downward from roots to ends to stop frizz and make hair shiny.
  2. Use the Brush to Lift: Pull the hair up with a comb or skeleton brush to give the hair height and volume.
  3. Use the Cold Button: Blast the styled hair with cold air for 5 seconds to lock the shape in place.

Lesson 6: Step-by-Step Taper Fade Guide

Lesson 6 of 9

A Taper is different from an all-around fade. A taper only fades down to bare skin at the sideburns and the bottom of the neck, leaving the hair around the ears dark and full.

How to Do a Clean Low Taper (Step-by-Step):

  1. Step 1 (Sideburn Line): Take your trimmer and make a straight horizontal line at the bottom of the sideburn.
  2. Step 2 (No Guard Open): Open the clipper lever all the way. Cut up half an inch. Move the lever halfway to blend the bottom line away.
  3. Step 3 (#1 Guard): Put on the #1 guard with the lever open. Cut up another half inch.
  4. Step 4 (#0.5 Guard): Put on the small #0.5 guard. Tap and flick the remaining dark spot until it looks smooth.
  5. Step 5 (The Neckline): Do the exact same steps at the bottom of the neck, fading from skin up into the hair.
  6. Step 6 (Edge-up): Use the corner of your trimmer to make the curved temple line (C-cup) crisp and sharp.

Lesson 7: Men's Haircut Catalog (12 Styles & Steps)

Lesson 7 of 9

Here are the 12 most requested men's haircuts. Each card shows a clear head-focused image and the exact steps to cut it.

Men's Cut

1. Low Skin Fade

The fade starts very low, right above the ear and drops low across the back nape.

How to Cut It:
  1. Make skin line 0.5" above ear using trimmer.
  2. Shave stubble underneath with foil shaver.
  3. Blend upward using lever open, then #0.5 guard.
  4. Connect to top with #1.5 and #2 guards.
Best For:Oval, Oblong Faces
Product:Matte Clay (Low Shine)[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

2. Mid Skin Fade

The balanced shop standard. Starts 1 inch above the ear across the back bump.

How to Cut It:
  1. Set bald line 1 inch above the ear with trimmer.
  2. Shave bald with foil shaver.
  3. Flick out lines using #0 Open → #0.5 → #1 guards.
  4. Scissor-cut top hair and blend into sides.
Best For:Round, Square, Oval
Product:Midnight Matte Clay[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

3. High Skin Fade

Faded very high near the top corners of the head. High contrast and clean.

How to Cut It:
  1. Set skin line up at the temple corner.
  2. Clear all lower hair down to bare skin.
  3. Blend fast within 1 inch using #0.5 and #1 guards.
  4. Keep top hair thick or textured.
Best For:Round Faces (Slims face)
Product:Texture Dust Powder[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

4. Clean Low Taper

Faded skin only at the sideburn and back neck. Keeps natural side bulk.

How to Cut It:
  1. Fade sideburns from skin up to #1 guard.
  2. Fade bottom neck from skin up to #2 guard.
  3. Do not cut the hair behind the ears.
  4. Make crisp C-cup lines with razor and gel.
Best For:Students & Office workers
Product:Classic Hair Pomade[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

5. Military Buzz Cut

Short uniform length on top with a skin fade on the sides. Zero daily styling.

How to Cut It:
  1. Cut whole top with #2 or #3 guard against grain.
  2. Put a mid or high skin fade on the sides.
  3. Make the forehead lineup laser-straight with trimmers.
  4. Finish perimeter with razor and shaving gel.
Best For:Strong jawline, Athletes
Product:Scalp Lotion or Beard Oil[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

6. Modern Flow Mullet

Clean, faded temple sides with longer, wavy hair flowing down the neck.

How to Cut It:
  1. Clip and separate the long back hair.
  2. Taper or fade only the sideburns.
  3. Trim back hair with scissors, keeping it 3–5" long.
  4. Texture the top so it sweeps naturally back.
Best For:Diamond, Oval, Heart
Product:Sea Salt Spray[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

7. French Crop

Top hair is combed forward with a straight fringe line across the forehead.

How to Cut It:
  1. Do a high skin fade on back and sides.
  2. Comb all top hair forward to the face.
  3. Point-cut with scissors to create chunky texture.
  4. Cut straight blunt fringe across the forehead.
Best For:Receding hairlines, Oval
Product:Volumizing Matte Powder[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

8. Classic Side Part

Gentleman's haircut parted cleanly on one side with tapered edges.

How to Cut It:
  1. Find the natural part line above the eyebrow.
  2. Taper the sides using #2 down to #1 guard.
  3. Scissor-cut the top, keeping the front fringe longest.
  4. Clean part line with straight razor if requested.
Best For:Professional & Formal style
Product:High Shine Water Pomade[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

9. The Burst Fade

A circular fade curving around the ear, leaving bulk behind the neck.

How to Cut It:
  1. Make a half-circle bald guideline around the ear.
  2. Foil-shave bottom edge right next to ear.
  3. Blend outward in a circle using #0.5 and #1 guards.
  4. Scissor-blend into the back neck hair.
Best For:Curly hair, Athletic look
Product:Curl Cream / Matte Paste[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

10. The Edgar Cut

Sharp, straight-across blunt bangs on the forehead with high skin-tapered sides.

How to Cut It:
  1. Execute a high taper or mid drop fade on sides.
  2. Comb top and front hair completely flat forward.
  3. Cut laser-straight blunt line across the mid-forehead.
  4. Use trimmer to sharpen the temple edge.
Best For:Oval, Oblong, Street Style
Product:Matte Styling Powder[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

11. Slick Back Undercut

Top hair is long and combed straight back, disconnected from short buzzed sides.

How to Cut It:
  1. Section top hair in a horseshoe parting and clip up.
  2. Buzz sides and back with #1 or #2 guard straight up.
  3. Trim top hair to 4–5" uniform length with shears.
  4. Blow-dry straight back using warm heat.
Best For:Strong jawline, Square faces
Product:Medium-Shine Classic Pomade[cite: 2]
Men's Cut

12. Modern Crew Cut

Classic short haircut graduated from longer in the front fringe to shorter at the crown.

How to Cut It:
  1. Taper or fade sides from #1 up to #2.5 guard.
  2. Cut crown short (0.5") using scissor-over-comb.
  3. Gradually leave front fringe longer (1.25" to 1.5").
  4. Brush front up and over into a mini pompadour.
Best For:Oval, Round, Conservative
Product:Matte Styling Paste[cite: 2]

Lesson 8: Women's Haircut Catalog (6 Styles & Steps)

Lesson 8 of 9

Modern barbers and stylists frequently service female clients looking for short-to-medium precision cuts. Below are the 6 foundational women's styles.

Women's Cut

1. Classic French / Jawline Bob

A sharp, solid haircut cut blunt along the jawline with zero layers.

How to Cut It:
  1. Section hair into 4 quadrants (center part + ear-to-ear).
  2. Have client tilt chin down slightly.
  3. Cut first guideline at the nape at 0° (zero elevation).
  4. Drop sections 0.5" at a time and cut flat to the line.
Best For:Oval, Heart, Fine hair
Product:Shine Hair Serum / Argan Oil
Women's Cut

2. Long Butterfly Layers

Flowing layers that create short bouncy volume on top while keeping length at the bottom.

How to Cut It:
  1. Separate top crown section from the bottom length.
  2. Pull top hair up at 90° and point-cut to remove weight.
  3. Over-direct front sections forward to frame the face.
  4. Round-brush blow-dry away from face for bounce.
Best For:Round, Square, Long hair
Product:Volumizing Mousse / Heat Spray
Women's Cut

3. Textured Pixie Cut

Short, feminine cropped cut tapered tightly around the ears and neck with textured top pieceyness.

How to Cut It:
  1. Taper back neck and around ears using shears or #3 guard.
  2. Elevate top sections at 90° using vertical partings.
  3. Deep point-cut top hair to create soft feathered tips.
  4. Soften sideburn points flat to the cheekbones.
Best For:Oval, Heart, Chiseled jaw
Product:Matte Styling Pomade / Wax
Women's Cut

4. Modern Shag & Curtain Bangs

Heavily layered haircut featuring cheek-framing curtain bangs parted down the middle.

How to Cut It:
  1. Section triangular bang area from center part to temple.
  2. Twist bang section once in center and slide-cut at 45°.
  3. Slide-cut side layers downward to connect into bangs.
  4. Diffuse or blow-dry with round brush curling outward.
Best For:Oblong, Oval, Wavy hair
Product:Sea Salt Spray / Texture Mist
Women's Cut

5. A-Line Graduated Lob

Collarbone-length bob that is shorter in the back and gently angles longer toward the front.

How to Cut It:
  1. Establish baseline at the nape sitting above shoulders.
  2. Cut diagonal-forward partings from back to front.
  3. Over-direct front sections backward slightly to retain length.
  4. Flat iron smooth with slight inward bevel.
Best For:Round, Square faces (Slims neck)
Product:Heat Protectant & Anti-Frizz Oil
Women's Cut

6. Blunt Collarbone Cut

Crisp, clean medium-length haircut sitting flat right at the collarbone with dense, thick ends.

How to Cut It:
  1. Comb damp hair flat with zero elevation (0°).
  2. Cut straight horizontal line using comb spine as ruler.
  3. Have client look straight ahead with shoulders relaxed.
  4. Point-cut vertical snips into ends to relieve tension.
Best For:All face shapes, Thick or fine hair
Product:Lightweight Styling Serum

Lesson 9: Tool Care, Zero-Gapping & Station Setup

Lesson 9 of 9

How to Oil Your Clippers (The 3-Drop Rule):

Clean hair out with a brush first. Put 3 drops of oil across the blade teeth and 1 drop on each back corner before and after every client. This keeps the clipper cool, sharp, and running fast.

How to Zero-Gap Your Clipper:

  1. Loosen the two back screws on the bottom blade slightly with a screwdriver.
  2. Slide the moving blade up so the teeth sit 0.5mm behind the front teeth.
  3. DANGER: The moving blade must NEVER stick out past the front blade, or it will cut the client's skin.
  4. Tighten the screws evenly while checking that the blades are straight.
Station Setup Checklist Before Starting:

Clean cape ready • White neck strip loaded • Blue Barbicide jar filled • Sharp razor blade ready in shavette • Clippers oiled and charged.
