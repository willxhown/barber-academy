Lesson 1: Foundations, Principles & Scissor Handling

Module 1 of 11

A good haircut is the foundation of every style. To be a true professional, you must master hair shaping, clean sectioning, precision continuity, and balance.

The 9 Factors Before Starting Any Cut:

  1. Head Shape: Check for flat spots, bumps, or uneven bone structure.
  2. Facial Contours: Balance round, square, or long face shapes.
  3. Neckline: Natural growth direction, cowlicks, and width.
  4. Hair Texture: Coarse, medium, fine, or wiry strands.
  5. Hair Density: Amount of hair per square inch (thick vs. thin).
  6. Hair Condition: Porosity, elasticity, and previous chemical damage.
  7. Client Occupation: Professional, student, athletic, or corporate requirements.
  8. Lifestyle & Maintenance: How much time the client spends styling daily.
  9. Personal Style: Clothing, aesthetic, and individuality.

How to Hold Shears (Scissors) Properly:

  • Ring Finger: Insert into the still finger ring of the shear.
  • Pinky Finger: Rest on the finger tang (brace) for balance and stabilization.
  • Thumb: Insert only the tip into the movable blade ring. Only the thumb moves; all other fingers stay still.
  • Index & Middle Fingers: Rest along the shank near the pivot screw to provide steady guiding control.

Palming the Shears with the Comb:

Never place your shears down on the counter while combing. This slows you down:

  • When Combing: Slip your thumb out of the ring, fold the shear blades closed into your palm, and hold the comb between thumb and index fingers of your dominant hand.
  • When Cutting: Transfer the comb to your non-dominant hand, re-insert your thumb into the shear ring, and cut with steady tension.

Lesson 4: Trichology (The Science of Hair & Scalp)

Module 4 of 11

Hair is an outgrowth of the skin made of the tough fibrous protein called Keratin. Because hair contains no nerves, clients feel zero pain when cutting it.

1. The Two Main Divisions of Hair:

  • Hair Root: The living portion below the scalp surface enclosed within the hair follicle. Contains the hair bulb, the dermal papilla (the "mother" of hair that supplies blood and nutrients), the arrector pili muscle (causes goosebumps), and sebaceous (oil) glands that secrete natural sebum.
  • Hair Shaft: The non-living portion extending above the skin surface that barbers cut and style.

2. The Three Layers of the Hair Shaft:

Layer NameLocation & StructureFunction in Barbering
CuticleOutermost protective layer of transparent overlapping scales.Protects interior. High heat or harsh chemicals open these scales, causing frizz.
CortexFibrous middle layer containing 90% of total hair weight.Gives hair strength, elasticity, and contains melanin pigment granules (hair color).
MedullaInnermost marrow/core of round cells.Found in thick, coarse hair; frequently absent in fine and naturally blonde hair.

3. Cross-Section Shapes & Natural Growth:

  • Straight Hair: Round cross-section (grows straight out of follicle).
  • Wavy Hair: Oval cross-section (produces natural wave and movement).
  • Curly / Kinky Hair: Flat, ribbon-like cross-section (curves tightly as it exits the scalp).

4. The 3 Phases of Hair Growth:

  1. Anagen Phase (Growth): Active division in the bulb lasting 2 to 7 years. 85–90% of scalp hair is in this stage.
  2. Catagen Phase (Transition): Follicle shrinks and detaches from the papilla over 1 to 2 weeks.
  3. Telogen Phase (Resting & Shedding): Resting stage lasting 2 to 4 months before shedding. Losing 50 to 80 hairs a day is completely normal.
Natural Hair Growth Directions:
• Hair Stream: Hair flowing naturally in the same direction.
• Whorl: Hair growing in a circular swirl around the crown.
• Cowlick: A stubborn tuft of hair standing straight up (often at the front hairline or crown). Always cut cowlicks with zero tension!

2. Shampoo Basin Protocol:

  1. Seat client comfortably and ask them to remove eyewear, jewelry, and tuck shirt collars inside.
  2. Test water temperature on your own inner wrist before wetting the client's hair.
  3. Use the soft pads of your fingers in circular motions. Never scratch the scalp with sharp fingernails.
  4. Cup your hand over the forehead to keep water and lather from entering the client's eyes.

3. Directional Blow-Drying:

  • Point Downward: Always direct airflow down the hair shaft (root to tip) to smooth the cuticles and eliminate frizz.
  • Lift Roots: Use a skeleton vent brush or round brush pulled upward at 90° to build root volume.
  • Cool Shot Lock: Blast warm-styled sections with cold air for 5 seconds to lock the final shape into place.

Lesson 6: Face Shapes & Head Measurement Technique

Module 6 of 11

A master haircut balances the client's face shape, bringing any head structure closer to the balanced ideal: the Square / Oval silhouette.

How to Measure a Client's Face:

  1. Forehead Width: Measure across the face from the peak of one eyebrow arch to the opposite arch.
  2. Cheekbones: Measure across the widest point, starting from the pointiest bone below the outer corner of each eye.
  3. Jawline: Measure from the tip of the chin up to the corner of the jaw below the ear, and multiply by 2.
  4. Face Length: Measure vertically from the center point of the front hairline down to the tip of the chin.

Face Shape Diagnosis & Style Recommendations:

Face ShapeMeasurement ResultsRecommended HaircutsStyles to Avoid
Oval Length is greater than cheekbones; forehead is wider than round jaw. Mid Skin Fade, Pompadour, Slick Back, Tapers. Heavy, flat bangs covering the forehead.
Square All measurements are nearly equal with a sharp, angular jawline. Buzz Cut, Side Part, French Crop, High Taper. Blunt, boxy fringes that over-widen the jaw.
Round Cheekbones and face length are equal with a soft, curved jawline. High Skin Fade, Spiky Quiff, Pompadour (adds height). Low tapers with wide side bulk; round bowl cuts.
Oblong / Long Face length is noticeably the largest measurement; cheeks flat. Low Taper Fade, Layered Scissor Cut, Side Part with volume. Extreme vertical pompadours that stretch the head.
Diamond Widest at cheekbones with narrow forehead and pointed chin. Textured French Crop, Flow Mullet, Scissor Side Part. High bald fades that make temples look overly narrow.
Heart / Triangle Wide forehead and narrow chin (or narrow forehead with wide jaw). Crew Cut, Textured Quiff, Mid Fade with fringe, Comb-over. Severe buzz cuts that emphasize unbalanced width.

Lesson 7: Step-by-Step Tapers & Core Barber Techniques

Module 7 of 11

A Taper Fade blends hair down to skin strictly at the sideburns and the nape, preserving natural bulk around the ears and perimeter.

Step-by-Step Low Taper Protocol:

  1. Sideburn Baseline: Place the trimmer horizontally at the bottom of the sideburn (level with earlobe). Make a clean 0.25-inch skin line.
  2. First Transition: Open the clipper lever all the way (no guard). Cut upward 0.5 inches. Move lever halfway to blend away the bottom line.
  3. Guard Ladder: Attach the #1 guard (lever open), cut upward another 0.5 inches. Attach the #0.5 guard (lever half) to flick out the dark shadow.
  4. Nape Taper: Make a horizontal trimmer skin line across the bottom 0.5 inches of the neckline. Foil-shave the lowest stubble.
  5. Nape Blending: Work upward using lever open → #0.5 guard → #1 guard → #2 guard, blending into the occipital bulk.
  6. C-Cup Detailing: Stretch temple skin taut with your non-dominant thumb. Carve the C-cup curve using only the outer corner of the trimmer blade, finishing with a straight razor at 30°.

The 4 Essential Hand Techniques:

  • Scissor-Over-Comb: Maintain a square vertical shape on the sides and back. Work in smooth, ascending columns until the comb glides off the head curve.
  • Clipper-Over-Comb: Fast, efficient debulking. The angle of the comb dictates the shape; swipe the clipper cleanly across the comb spine.
  • Shaping Up (Edge-Up): Never push back or eat into the natural hairline! Always preserve maximum fullness and detail with a straight razor.
  • Texturizing: Point-cut vertically into layers with shears or single-notched thinning scissors to remove weight and enhance movement.

Short, feminine cropped cut tapered tightly around the ears and neck with textured top pieceyness.

Heavily layered haircut featuring cheek-framing curtain bangs parted down the middle.

Collarbone-length bob that is shorter in the back and gently angles longer toward the front.

Crisp, clean medium-length haircut sitting flat right at the collarbone with dense, thick ends.

Lesson 10: Care & Maintenance of Shears & Clippers

Module 10 of 11

1. Daily Shear Care Protocol:

  1. Handle with Extreme Care: Never drop shears on counters. Even a micro-bend damages blade alignment. Use shears strictly on clean hair; cutting paper or tape dulls edges instantly.
  2. Cleaning & Disinfection: Wipe shears between clients with a microfiber cloth and 70% alcohol. Soak non-porous tools in blue Barbicide liquid for 10 minutes.
  3. Tension Adjustment: Hold shears vertically by the finger ring. Lift thumb ring to 90° and let drop. If it drops shut, it is too loose (hair bends instead of cutting). If it stays open, it is too tight (causes hand fatigue). It should stop smoothly at 45°.
  4. Daily Pivot Oiling: Open shears to 45°, place one drop of shear oil at the pivot screw, and leave overnight. Wipe away hair and debris in the morning.

2. Electric Clipper Maintenance:

  • The 3-Drop Rule: Brush out hair. Apply 3 drops across teeth and 1 drop on each back rail before and after every client.
  • Daily Blade Wash: Pour blade wash into a shallow dish. Submerge only the moving teeth with clippers running for 15–20 seconds to flush deeply lodged hair and dirt.

3. Clipper Zero-Gapping Step-by-Step:

  1. Loosen the two rear screws on the still blade by half a turn.
  2. Slide moving blade up so cutting teeth sit 0.1mm to 0.5mm behind the still blade edge.
  3. SAFETY WARNING: Never allow the moving blade to extend past the still blade. An overlapping moving blade will pinch and cut client skin!
  4. Tighten screws evenly while checking alignment on a flat mirror surface.

Lesson 11: Launching a Barbershop & Operations Infrastructure

Module 11 of 11

Transitioning from an employed barber to a shop owner requires spatial planning, legal licensing, and connected digital masterfiles to manage staff commissions, booking schedules, and inventory.

1. Station Dimensions & Physical Layout:

  • Chair Clearance: Maintain a minimum of 4.5 to 5 linear feet between the centers of each barber chair.
  • Working Radius: Keep at least 4 to 5 feet of clear space behind each chair to allow the barber to navigate comfortably.
  • Mirror Lighting: Install diffuse 4000K–5000K daylight-balanced LED lighting at eye level on both sides of the mirror to eliminate shadows around the client's neck and ears.

2. Philippine Business Compliance Checklist:

Step / PermitIssuing AgencyOperational Requirement
1. Trade Name RegistrationDTI (Sole Proprietorship) or SEC (Corp)Registers and protects the brand name (e.g., Wilsons Midnight Barbers).
2. Barangay Business ClearanceLocal Barangay HallAuthorizes commercial operations within the local barangay jurisdiction.
3. Mayor's / Business PermitCity / Municipal Hall (BPLO)Annual operating license; requires fire safety, building, and electrical clearances.
4. BIR Registration (Form 2303)Bureau of Internal RevenueCertificate of Registration, printing of Official Receipts (OR), and POS registration.
5. Sanitation Permit & Health CardsCity Health OfficeAnnual sanitary inspection and health cards (chest X-ray, stool exam) for all barbers.

3. Operational Databases (The Three Core Masterfiles):

  1. Employee Masterfile: Contains staff contact information, hiring records, and commission split percentages. Links directly to Daily Time Records (DTR) and automated payroll math.
  2. Service Masterfile: Sets standardized service prices, point values, and duration intervals (e.g., Fade = 45 min, Hot Towel = 30 min) to prevent scheduling overlaps.
  3. Product Masterfile: Distinguishes Retail inventory from Backbar shop supplies. Automatically updates stock counts and triggers reorder warnings when counts drop below thresholds.
