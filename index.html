Chapter 1: Foundations, Principles & Scissor Hair Shaping

Form Geometry, Elevation Angles & Scissor Mechanics

Haircutting is structural architecture applied to an organic, curved surface. Mastery requires strict control of angles, tension, and shear biomechanics.

1. The Four Fundamental Haircutting Forms

Form StructureElevation AngleWeight DistributionVisual Result
Solid Form (Blunt Cut)0° (Natural Fall)Maximum weight at the perimeter line.Solid horizontal/diagonal edge; zero graduation.
Graduated Form (Wedge)1° to 89° (Commonly 45°)Weight builds up along an interior ridge.Stacked, bevelled volume with visible contour.
Uniformly Layered Form90° Straight from scalpEqual length throughout the head silhouette.Rounded contour; weight is dispersed evenly.
Progressive (Square) Layers90° to 180° ElevatedShorter interior crown, longer perimeters.Masculine, flat top planes; removes bulky crowns.

2. Parting & Elevation Principles

  • Horizontal Partings: Build weight and stability; used for baselines and perimeter blunts.
  • Vertical Partings: Remove weight and create flat, vertical planes; used for layering and graduation.
  • Diagonal Forward / Back: Directs hair movement either toward or away from the face.
  • Tension Dynamics: Wet hair stretches up to 40–50%. Use minimum-to-zero tension over cowlicks, ears, and receding hairlines to prevent cut-backs.

3. Advanced Scissor Hair Shaping Techniques

Chapter 4: Proper Draping, Shampooing & Ergonomic Blow Drying

Client Care, Water Temperature & Tension Direction
  1. Double-Towel Draping: Place the first towel around the neck, apply the cape over the towel (ensuring no cape plastic touches skin), and fold a second clean towel over the cape collar. For dry cuts, use an elastic paper neck strip pulled taut and folded outward.
  2. Shampoo Basin Mechanics: Test water on your inner wrist before touching the scalp. Use the finger pads (never fingernails) in circular motions, working from temples back to the occipital bone.
  3. Ergonomic Blow-Drying: Always point the airflow nozzle downward along the cuticle (from root to tip). Lift roots at 90° using a vent brush, apply warm heat to set structure, and lock in shape with a 5-second cold shot.

Chapter 6: Step-by-Step Taper Masterclass (Low, Mid & High)

Fading Mechanics for Natural Edge-Ups

A taper fades hair down to the skin exclusively at the sideburns and the nape, preserving full weight and natural hairlines around the ear perimeters.

Low Taper Execution Protocol

  1. Sideburn Baseline: Place detailing trimmer horizontally at the lower bottom of the sideburn (level with the earlobe). Create a 0.25-inch skin line.
  2. First Transition: Clipper lever open (no guard), cut upward 0.5 inches. Close lever to halfway and flick out the baseline.
  3. Guard Ladder: Attach #1 guard (lever open), cut upward another 0.5 inches. Attach #0.5 guard (lever halfway) to erase the remaining dark line.
  4. Nape Taper Baseline: Set a skin guideline across the bottom 0.5 inches of the neckline with trimmers. Foil-shave the lowest stubble.
  5. Nape Blending: Work upward using lever open → #0.5 guard → #1 guard → #2 guard, blending into the occipital bone weight.
  6. C-Cup Edge-Up: Stretch temple skin taut with the non-dominant thumb. Carve the C-cup curve using only the outer corner of the trimmer blade, finishing with a straight razor at 30°.

Chapter 8: Maintenance & Care of Shears, Clippers & Razors

Sanitation, Zero-Gapping & Lubrication Science

1. Daily Scissor Maintenance Protocol

  1. Cleaning: At the end of each shift, wipe blades thoroughly using a soft microfiber cloth with 70% isopropyl alcohol to remove hair, styling chemicals, and moisture.
  2. Tension Adjustment: Hold the shears by the finger ring vertically. Lift the thumb ring to 90° and let it drop. If it drops completely shut, it is too loose. If it stays completely open, it is too tight. It should stop smoothly at approximately 45°.
  3. Oiling the Pivot: Place one small drop of shear oil directly into the pivot screw point daily. Open and close the shears several times to disperse the oil and wipe away excess.

2. Clipper Maintenance & The 3-Drop Rule

Always brush out hair from the blade teeth after every service. Apply three drops of clipper oil across the cutting teeth and one drop on each rear rail heel before and after every client. Test blade temperature against your inner wrist every 10 minutes to protect clients from thermal discomfort.

3. Clipper Zero-Gapping Step-by-Step

  1. Loosen the two screws on the still blade by half a turn.
  2. Slide the moving blade upward until its teeth sit 0.1 mm to 0.5 mm behind the still blade edge.
  3. SAFETY RULE: The moving blade must never extend past the still blade. An overlapping moving blade will pinch and cut the client's skin.
  4. Tighten the screws evenly while holding the blades parallel against a flat surface.

Chapter 9: Launching a Barbershop & Operations Infrastructure

Physical Ergonomics, Philippine Permits & Operational Databases

Opening a barbershop requires structured spatial planning, local legal registration, and connected digital masterfiles to manage staff commissions, booking schedules, and inventory[cite: 1, 2].

1. Station Dimensions & Physical Layout

  • Chair Clearance: Maintain a minimum of 4.5 to 5 linear feet between the centers of each barber chair.
  • Working Radius: Keep at least 4 to 5 feet of clear space behind each chair to allow the barber to navigate comfortably.
  • Mirror Lighting: Install diffuse 4000K–5000K daylight-balanced LED lighting at eye level on both sides of the mirror to eliminate shadows around the client's neck and ears.

2. Philippine Business Compliance Checklist

3. Operational Databases (The Three Core Masterfiles)

  1. Employee Masterfile: Contains staff contact information, hiring records, and commission split percentages[cite: 2]. Links directly to Daily Time Records (DTR) and automated payroll math[cite: 1, 2].
  2. Service Masterfile: Sets standardized service prices, point values, and duration intervals (e.g., Fade = 45 min, Hot Towel = 30 min) to prevent scheduling overlaps[cite: 2].
  3. Product Masterfile: Distinguishes Retail inventory from Backbar shop supplies[cite: 2]. Automatically updates stock counts and triggers reorder warnings when counts drop below thresholds[cite: 1, 2].
